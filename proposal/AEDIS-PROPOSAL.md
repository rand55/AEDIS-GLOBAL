## PART 0 — PURPOSE AND SCOPE

**0.1 What AEDIS is for.** The health, education, shelter, water, food safety and honest
information of every human being alive. Capacity that exists because people need it, not
because they can pay for it.

**0.2 What AEDIS is not for.** Enriching a particular enterprise, making any state the
dominant military power, or preserving any institution because it is incumbent. A framework
that could be captured for those purposes has failed at the first step.

**0.3 The premise.** Machine labour is absorbing work faster than new work is created, and
is no longer confined to routine manufacturing. Haulage, field agriculture, cleaning,
warehousing, clerical work, logistics and parts of medicine are being automated with
improving economics. The job does not vanish; it moves up one level, and one trained person
now operates what many used to. That trajectory does not reverse, and designing for its
reversal is designing for a world that will not arrive.

**0.4 The standing constraint.** No person displaced by automation may be left in a position
where their remaining options are to fight the technology or to die. Every clause in this document
is answerable to one question: **does this leave some group with nothing left to lose?**

**0.4a Existing protection comes first, and AEDIS funds the gap.** Most countries already operate
unemployment insurance, sick pay and pension entitlements, financed by contributions from pay. That
is the first layer of the guarantee in 0.4, and **AEDIS does not replace it.** The guarantee is
satisfied by the national system plus the Tier 2 dividend on top of it, and only the uncovered
remainder is AEDIS money. This is the answer to the objection that 0.4 demanded an unfunded new
entitlement: in most places the entitlement already exists, and the framework's obligation is to
name it, count it and top it up.

**Where no such system exists, AEDIS helps build one rather than paying indefinitely around its
absence** (6.6).

**0.4b The gate holds on the supplement, not on the fund.** OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini
independently struck the earlier precondition, which required a *funded scheme* to exist before
Tier 2 intake opened. Their objection was the same and it was correct: in the jurisdictions least
able to build a scheme, that rule produces the outcome 0.4 forbids — compliance with the framework
becomes the reason someone has nothing.

The gate therefore holds on what a displaced person actually receives:

- Tier 2 intake opens once the 6.6 statute is enacted **and the transition supplement is paying at
  or above the 11.1 subsistence baseline**;
- the combined value — national scheme, transition supplement, Tier 2 dividend — is what must clear
  the baseline, and the ledger operator verifies it before intake opens;
- **until the national fund reaches its certified reserve, the treasury funds interim subsistence for
  anyone the payroll base does not reach**, including informal-sector workers who never contributed
  to a scheme they never had.

A jurisdiction cannot be excluded for being poor. It can be required to pay a supplement it can
afford, which is what the Tier 1 envelope is for.

**0.5 The objective.** AI and humans working together, with gains distributed broadly enough
that the arrangement holds. Not AI replacing people; not people suppressing AI. A division of
labour that survives its own success.

**0.6 Two commitments that bind AEDIS against its own friends.**

- **Publish adverse findings** with the same prominence as successes. If an audit, ruling,
  stress test or monitor finds AEDIS is failing, it is published. If a treasury, contractor,
  bank or central bank is the cause, it is named.
- **Submit to the same test it applies to others.** Where AEDIS fails the criteria it sets
  for others, the failure is stated in this document rather than left to a critic to find.
  Part XI is that list, and it is meant to grow.

## PART I — ARCHITECTURE: THREE TIERS

AEDIS is three programmes with separate envelopes, separate accounting and separate
governance. Treating them as one is what produced the contradiction in earlier drafts, which
capped operating costs at 24 months while promising to pay every clinician indefinitely.

**1.1 Tier 1 — Recurrent, permanent.**
Medical and dental care for every human, with no time limit. Clinician salaries, including
newly qualified practitioners leaving training. Relocation support where a region lacks the
people it needs. **This is permanent money. It does not retire, and it is never described as
asset-backed.** It is bounded by an envelope and governed by the monetary operating standard
in 3.6.

**1.2 Tier 2 — Transition, time-limited per event.**
A training dividend, capped at **24 months per verified displacement event** (6.1), for
workers displaced by automation and for people entering occupations that are not a public
need. Eligibility accepts either verified displacement or a qualifying occupational transition
under published criteria, so a person this clause covers cannot fail the authorisation gate for
want of a displacement. It self-liquidates: each event's support ends. It does not apply to
clinical training, where qualification ends training and begins Tier 1 employment.

**1.3 Tier 3 — Capital, asset-linked.**
Assets and their renewal: hospitals, clinics, schools, water, energy, transport, housing,
sanitation, research facilities. Classified revenue-bearing or non-revenue at approval. Only
revenue-bearing assets return money to the ledger.

**1.4 No tier may borrow from another, and the rule is enforced, not declared.** Tier 3
receipts cannot fund Tier 1 care; Tier 1 issuance cannot be counted as Tier 3 retirement. Any
transfer between tiers is a published amendment to this document, not a budget decision. **The ledger protocol blocks cross-tier transfers automatically and publishes every attempt, and a treasury reducing baseline spending below its published figure suspends new approvals under 4.4.** A rule without an automatic block is a suggestion.

**Every baseline schedule published under 1.4 must carry a real-intensity metric, and the metric
must be one the treasury cannot choose alone.**

Nominal spending can be held on schedule while the service behind it shrinks: the money is relabelled,
the function is quietly replaced, or the count is inflated. A schedule that can only be met by
spending more is not a measurable commitment, so:

- **the lot-drawn auditor holds two frozen metrics per covered function, one of volume and one of
  access or quality**, and the definitions are published at activation. Choosing a single metric is
  what makes the test gameable: a count of connections held up while the connections fail is a
  passing number attached to a failing service;
- **the choice belongs to the auditor, not the treasury**, because a treasury that selects its own
  scorecard has not been measured;
- **a breach exists when nominal spending is at or above the published schedule while either frozen
  metric falls beyond the published tolerance for two consecutive observation periods**, and a breach
  flag opens a substitution finding within sixty days rather than a suspension. The finding asks what
  replaced what, and an expired deadline confers no clearance;
- **metrics are need-adjusted before they are judged.** Falling patient encounters because a
  population got healthier is not a breach, and a rule that cannot tell the difference punishes
  success;
- **a statistical revision is permitted only with a reproducible historical bridge, published before
  the revision takes effect**, because a series that can be redefined after ratification is a series
  whose author controls the result.

The same test applies to the spare-capacity component of the 3.7 envelope. **The sectoral
unemployment and capital-utilisation series used in that denominator may not be redefined after
ratification** except under the bridge rule above, and an independent ruling on the bridge is
published with it. Whoever can redefine the denominator can silently raise the ceiling.

**The baseline is a published spending schedule, not a number.** Before activation the treasury
publishes an independently audited schedule stating the baseline for each covered function, its
scope, its price adjustment and the demographic changes it permits. **Relabelling a line, or nominal
growth that conceals real substitution, counts as a breach of the baseline** — otherwise the
suspension can be defeated by renaming an account. OpenAI's failure test: relabelled or nominally
inflated spending conceals real substitution without triggering suspension.

## PART II — THE MONETARY FOUNDATION: SEIGNIORAGE, NOT DEBT

**2.1 AEDIS is not a loan and does not create debt.** No maturity, no contractual interest, no
holder may redeem it. Settlement nevertheless creates remunerated central-bank reserves, and
2.6 allocates that cost rather than leaving it off the books. Base money behaves the same way in every major currency: it is a central bank
liability that is never repaid, and the stock grows roughly with nominal GDP. AEDIS models
SIC on that, rather than on sovereign borrowing.

**2.2 Therefore the question is not who repays. It is who directs.** Every central bank
already creates base money. After 2008 and 2020 the Federal Reserve's balance sheet grew from
under $1 trillion to roughly $9 trillion, directed overwhelmingly through bank reserves and
asset purchases, which supported the prices of existing financial assets. That is
seigniorage — a transfer of purchasing power to the issuer — and its destination was set by
institutions rather than by any published rule.

**2.3 What AEDIS changes is the destination, not the mechanism.** New money is directed at
verified capacity and permanent care, allocated by published rule, auditable on a ledger.
The envelope bounds the amount; the rule bounds the destination. A rule with no envelope is
hyperinflation. An envelope with no rule is the present arrangement.

**2.4 The cost is real and is disclosed.** Seigniorage is paid by holders of money through
eroded purchasing power whenever issuance outpaces what the economy can absorb. There is no
version of this where nobody bears any cost. The precise, testable question is: **does the
real cost of essentials fall faster than the currency dilutes?** If it does, the poorest are
net winners. If it does not, the programme has failed, and Part VIII requires that this be
published.

**2.5 AEDIS is structurally separate from the banking system, but not functionally
isolated.** It takes no deposits, runs no maturity transformation and creates no credit in
the ordinary sense; it authorises issuance and executes rule-bound payments against verified
milestones. It does settle into national currencies, because contractors must be paid in
money they can spend. That settlement interface is where convertibility, exchange-loss
allocation and legal classification must be specified (8.2).

**2.6 Settlement costs are allocated, not assumed away.** SIC bears no contractual interest, but
contractors deposit their payments in commercial banks, which expands central-bank reserves,
which are remunerated. The cost lands on the treasury through lower remittances whether or not
anyone books it. **Before activation the treasury and central bank shall publish a binding
agreement allocating reserve remuneration, sterilisation costs, foregone remittances, exchange
losses and recapitalisation obligations, with any unassigned exposure defaulting to the
treasury.** Consolidated accounts shall distinguish asset purchases from AEDIS transfers. The
agreement is published annually and reconciles to audited central-bank accounts; an unallocated
residual line is a failed audit.

## PART III — ISSUANCE RULES

**3.1 Productive issuance only.** Every issuance is classified at approval as *productive*
(asset-forming, capacity-augmenting, income-producing now or later) or *consumptive*
(military expenditure, patronage, legacy debt service, general budget support). **Only
productive issuance is eligible for AEDIS credit.**

**Training and care are productive by this definition, and saying so explicitly matters**,
because both are routinely described as spending. A trained worker augments capacity exactly as a
turbine does. What training does not produce is a *recoverable* asset — no claim exists on a skill,
and no holder can be made to repay it. **6.9 states what follows from that distinction**, and it is
the reason Tier 2 is financed as investment in one paragraph and treated as a transfer in the next. Consumptive expenditure remains on
conventional sovereign borrowing, where interest cost and market discipline already apply.

**3.2 The eligibility test is tier-specific**, published and falsifiable. Every authorisation
must cite its envelope.

- **Tier 1:** verified delivery of covered care.
- **Tier 2:** verified displacement or a qualifying occupational transition, plus verified
  training participation **or** verified deployment into work as the progress evidence (6.2).
  **Tier 2 also authorises interim continuity payment under 6.8(d) for up to 60 days pending a
  training start**, which is the exception that makes 6.8(d) lawful: without it, 3.2's participation
  gate makes paying a failed audit and 6.8(d) makes not paying one, and the ledger operator fails
  either way. Sixty days is a placeholder under Appendix D.
- **Tier 3:** the project adds measurable capacity; that capacity is verified before the next
  tranche; and the project would still be approved if its output had to be sold at cost.

A single asset-project test cannot authorise care or training; applying one was a category error
in an earlier draft. Equally, no non-asset tier may be forced through commercial cost-recovery
tests. An audit finding either defect is a failed audit.

**3.3 The humanitarian baseline.** SIC authorisation is void for military operations, combat
equipment or offensive weapons systems. Any attempt to divert credit to those purposes
permanently disqualifies the responsible entity and triggers automatic public disclosure.

**3.4 Issuance is released against verified progress, not against promises.**
- **Materials first.** Procurement is funded against delivered materials.
- **Labour when performed.** A tranche at the midpoint of construction, the balance at
  completion.
- **Training dividends against verified participation**, never credited in advance.
- Peak circulating SIC during construction is therefore roughly **30–50% of contract value**,
  not 100%.

**3.5 Two published figures per project, never one.**
- **Construction exposure** — peak unbacked circulating stock during build.
- **Terminal clearing horizon** — outstanding stock at handover divided by annual
  cancellation, measured from the date receipts begin.

Neither is a forecast. Both are required for every project, and the stock identity
`outstanding SIC = prior stock + actual issuance − actual cancellation` is published by
cohort. **Completion clears nothing.** There is no asset-backed clearing event: physical
completion neither extinguishes the currency issued to pay for it nor creates a redeemable
claim against the asset.

**3.6 The monetary operating standard.** Before activation, each participating central bank
publishes, for its currency area: the inflation measure and its basket; the observation
period; the issuance-adjustment rule; and the legally executable instruments available to
withdraw purchasing power.

- The basket and its methodology are **frozen at ratification** and independently audited.
  The body being constrained may not redefine the measure.
- Adjustment is **proportional** to the size of the breach and responds to forecasts, not only
  to realised inflation.
- The mechanism must be able to **cancel currency**, not merely delay issuance.
- It must account for construction and humanitarian commitments that cannot be paused.
- **On breach, the central bank alone — without treasury override — reduces in order: uncommitted
  Tier 3 approvals first, then new Tier 2 intakes. Existing care, paid dividends and protected
  commitments continue.** Where a Tier 2 intake restriction would leave a verified displaced person
below the subsistence baseline measured under 11.1, the central bank first reallocates an equal
nominal value from uncommitted Tier 3 revenue-bearing approvals; the envelope ceiling does not
rise to accommodate the exemption. **Whether an alternative is enforceable — meaning support at or above the 11.1 subsistence
baseline, not merely some payment — is determined under 7.4 within 30 days, and a missed deadline
defaults to the applicant.** Anthropic's point: two adjudicators asking two different questions is
how a displaced person ends up with neither answer. **If a breach persists after both steps — the reduction of uncommitted Tier 3 approvals and the reallocation that funds the exemption — the central bank executes the withdrawal instruments published under 3.7, at treasury cost allocated under 2.6.** Claude's point: an exemption that can swallow the second step leaves a persistent breach with no instrument at all.
- Participating nations submit audited historical input–output tables before activation.

**3.7 The envelope has an operational denominator, and cancellation capacity is a
precondition.** The annual envelope is **the lower of a published share of GDP or measured spare capacity
in receiving non-tradable sectors plus import absorption**, set and enforced by the central bank
alone. **Spare capacity is calculated from audited sectoral unemployment and capital utilisation
in non-tradables. Import absorption is the maximum current-account deficit serviceable without
breaching the exchange-rate pass-through limit published under 3.6.** Before an envelope is approved the monetary operator publishes a dated,
stressed cash-flow schedule: cancellation needs during construction and early operation;
executable withdrawal instruments with amounts, costs and implementation lags; and net receipts
under delayed completion, lower demand and higher operating costs. **An envelope that fails is
reduced before commitments are made** — never clawed back from spent advances. Raising a ceiling immediately after an inflation breach is a failed audit, not a calibration.

**3.7a The ceiling's denominator is frozen against the operator.** The spare-capacity and
import-absorption series that size the envelope are published at activation, and may be revised only
with a reproducible historical bridge and an independent ruling, both published before the revision
takes effect. A ceiling calculated from a series its author can redefine is not a ceiling.

**3.8 The cancellation waterfall.** All receipts from revenue-bearing assets are applied in
this order, automatically, on the ledger:

1. verified operating costs, capped by a schedule published at approval, **excluding any
   operating cost assigned to the national payer under 4.3**;
2. a maintenance reserve, capped and independently audited;
3. **a fixed 20% to the Local Renewal Schedule (4.7)**, held in a zero-interest central-bank
   sub-account;
4. **all remaining receipts are swept to the central bank to extinguish commercial-bank reserves
   and cancel an equal nominal amount of SIC.**

**No recycling, no stabilisation reserve and no discretionary holding — which means no off-ledger
surplus pools. Only scheduled operating, maintenance and 4.7 balances may be held.** **The central bank — not the settlement operator — publishes independent confirmation that
reserves were extinguished and an equal nominal amount of SIC cancelled. Matched entries must show
it; an account transfer alone does not qualify, and audited SIC outstanding must fall by the swept
amount.**

**3.9 Surplus-first, including interest on operational balances.** **Before any new credit
is authorised for a local work the ledger operator applies, first, the legally available operating
and maintenance balances — including interest accrued on them — and then the principal held in the
zero-interest 4.7 sub-account, which earns none by design.** Both are applied only above the
certified maintenance floor under 4.5. Interest is
revenue: it enters the same waterfall and follows the same split. Idle money cannot earn while
new currency is issued.

**3.10 Classification of assets.** Each asset is classified at approval:

- **Revenue-bearing** — energy generation and grids, water treatment and pipelines, telecoms,
  transit, ports, data centres, and commercial floor space inside public assets. These carry
  published tariffs or rents charged to **commercial users**, and their receipts follow 3.8.
- **Non-revenue** — clinics, schools, parks, shelter, rehabilitation facilities and other
  social infrastructure. These charge the public nothing, carry **no retirement schedule**, and
  are **not claimed as monetary backing**. Their issuance is permanent money under 3.6.

Classification is frozen at approval. Reclassification is an amendment to this document, not
a management decision.

**3.11 The Portfolio Retirement Ratio is published, not assumed.** Realised cancellations over
realised issuance, by asset class and in aggregate, alongside outstanding stock, with projected
receipts labelled separately and never presented as achieved. **No minimum ratio is imposed
until out-of-sample evidence supports one**; the figures currently in circulation are
placeholders, and this document does not pretend otherwise. **Every placeholder is listed in
Appendix D with its producer, its source and its deadline. No figure may be used operationally
while it remains undefined.**

**3.11a No retirement floor is recommended on in-sample evidence.** Before an auditor recommends a
class-specific floor, it preregisters a held-out test — the cohort, the matching variables and the
acceptance threshold — and the recommendation stands or falls on the held-out result.

**The denominator and the window are named, because all four panelists asked for them:** realised
cancellations over realised issuance, per asset class, measured over a **rolling sixty-month window
of completed assets of that class**, with assets excluded until twelve months after handover so that
receipts are in operation rather than start-up. A class with fewer than five completed assets in the
window has no ratio published and no floor recommended, and that fact is published rather than
averaged away. OpenAI, Claude
and DeepSeek each asked for this; a percentile derived from the same projects that justified it is
a description, not a test.

**3.12 The residual truth, stated rather than buried.** Revenue-bearing assets retire a real
but modest share of issuance: on the order of 8–12% of build cost per year for energy and
desalination, 5–10% for transit and ports, 0.4–1% for commercial space inside a hospital, and
**zero for social infrastructure.** Any claim that assets retire all issuance is false. A
partial valve described accurately is defensible; an overstated one is destroyed by a single
question.

## PART IV — TIER 3: CAPITAL DELIVERY

**4.1 Demand and staffing are proven before authorisation.** No asset is authorised without:

- **evidence of need** — enrolment registers, patient waiting lists, or pre-committed service
  agreements from the institutions that will use it;
- **committed staffing** — named practitioners with pre-ordered slots, commitment letters, and
  an identified training pipeline rather than an assumed one;
- **operations costed against actual fiscal capacity**, not promised.

An asset with no committed users or no committed staff is not authorised.

**4.2 One payer per cost line, assigned before authorisation.** Before construction, the
treasury and care purchaser publish a stress-tested, legally appropriated operating schedule
assigning every cost to exactly one payer. **Receipts committed under 3.8 may not be pledged
twice, and no asset's receipts may fund another asset's operations outside 4.7.** Treasuries
publish staffing and operating commitments stress-tested against revenue shortfalls.
Unaffordable projects are resized or deferred.

**4.3 The operating period is bounded, and the handover is funded rather than promised.**
AEDIS funds construction plus a declared startup period of **no more than 24 months,
non-renewable**. **Clinical salaries and covered-care fees remain Tier 1 permanently (1.1);
every other operating cost becomes the named national payer's responsibility, appropriated in
law before release.** **For revenue-bearing assets, receipts under 3.8(1) are the named payer; the national payer
covers only the appropriated shortfall.** The ledger operator withholds credit where a cost line is
claimed by both payers or by neither, and any such line blocks the drawdown automatically. **Extensions and reclassifications are ineligible for AEDIS issuance.**

**4.4 An unstaffed asset freezes new approvals** in that jurisdiction until it is staffed to
specification. **The ledger operator also freezes new approvals where audited spending on a covered
function falls below the baseline figure the treasury published at activation, until it is restored
(7.7 applies). The freeze excludes essential renewal certified by the independent assessor under
4.5.** Renewal claims require a condition assessment by an assessor the claimant does
not pay.

**4.5 Renewal is eligible capital, and the claim must be independently justified.** Repair and
replacement qualify on the same terms as new construction, subject to an assessor **the claimant
does not pay** verifying condition, examining maintenance history and comparing repair,
replacement and deferral. Remaining life under 36 months and cost under 60% of replacement are
**placeholders under 11.6**, not calibrations. **Until they are calibrated under 10.4, the assessor
approves renewal only where the net present value of five years of emergency repair and
service-disruption costs exceeds the renewal capital cost.**

Maintenance arrears trigger owner co-funding or escrow — **but essential repair is never
refused, and a distressed municipality is never locked out. Before activation the participating
treasury establishes a funded essential-repair backstop, from which an independent assessor
authorises emergency disbursement where co-funding or escrow cannot be obtained — without delaying
the repair and without waiving recovery from a solvent responsible owner.** That caveat exists because a hard test would
produce an outcome 0.4 forbids. **A framework that can build a school but cannot repair one has
not solved the problem it was created for.**

**4.6 Local content is an economic instrument, and it must earn its cost.** Projects carry an
**80% local labour preference** and a published local-materials preference. This is not sentiment:
income spent on imports multiplies abroad, and the multiplier is the mechanism by which construction
spending becomes local business revenue.

**The preference is void where it fails its own test, and the test has a decider.** Before bids
open, the independent procurement auditor rules whether **estimated domestic value-added exceeds the
unit-cost premium** over imported alternatives. Where it does not, the preference lapses
automatically for that contract and the ruling is published with its figures. A preference that
lapses or survives without an attributed ruling has not been tested at all.

All four panelists pressed on this clause and none of them accepted the first draft. DeepSeek
would strike the 80% preference entirely, calling it a subsidy to whoever already holds the local
franchise; OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini accepted it with the denominator and decider above. The
dissent is recorded rather than resolved.

**4.6a The capability-gap exception, which abolishes itself.** A local-labour preference that cannot
be waived would stop the projects it exists to enable: no country can build a grid it has never built
before using only people who have never built one. **An out-of-jurisdiction contractor or crew may
therefore be engaged where a capability-gap finding shows no local supplier or workforce able to do
the work**, on four published conditions:

1. **a finding, not an assertion** — the independent procurement auditor certifies the gap, naming
   the specific capability, before the bid is opened;
2. **named counterparts** — the contract obliges the contractor to train identified local workers, on
   a published schedule, with the training funded as 4.6 local content;
3. **a time limit** — the exception runs for a stated term, and the local-content requirement rises
   with each renewal;
4. **automatic lapse** — when the auditor finds the gap closed, the preference reverts to 4.6 in full
   and the exception cannot be renewed.

**The exception exists only to end itself.** A foreign contractor that arrives without a transfer
obligation converts a temporary gap into a permanent dependency, which is the outcome the local
preference was written to prevent. Cross-border work is the point; permanent exclusion of the local
workforce is the failure.

**4.6b When a country keeps importing the same equipment, build the factory.** A repeated-import test
runs against the procurement record: where a jurisdiction imports the same class of equipment for
three or more projects, **AEDIS may fund a manufacturing or assembly facility in that country as
Tier 3 capital.**

**Three imports show a pattern of demand, not a viable factory**, and OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini all
made that point. Beyond the count, authorisation therefore requires:

- **an aggregate volume threshold** — a stated minimum annual demand over a defined period, published
  before approval;
- **an audited unit-cost comparison against imports including freight, duty and lead time**, not
  against a list price;
- **a committed offtake for a stated share of the plant's output** under 4.13's rules, so the plant is
  not built on optimism;
- **a closure plan**, because a subsidised factory that cannot compete is a permanent draw. A facility serving the regional pipeline is revenue-bearing under 3.10 and retires
issuance from what it sells; a facility serving one project is non-revenue and is treated as such.

This is how a country stops paying freight and margin to someone else's factory. It is also the
cheapest form of local content: a workforce that can build the equipment is worth more than a
preference that guarantees it work. DeepSeek would have struck the preference entirely, on the
ground that it rewards local monopolies rather than competence; Gemini and Claude defended it. The
condition above is the compromise, and the dissent is recorded: a preference that cannot show its
multiplier is a subsidy to whoever already holds the local franchise.

**4.7 Local Renewal Allocation.** Of the surplus after operating costs and the maintenance
reserve, a **fixed 20%** funds a Local Renewal Schedule **fixed and costed before
construction**, audited independently:

- eligible works are those AEDIS would otherwise have to fund — road repair, clinic and school
  maintenance, water, drainage, safety;
- **new amenities with no public-need test are ineligible**;
- no official may vary the split or the schedule;
- balances accumulate within one schedule period and are cancelled at its end; unspent
  allocation is not carried forward;
- the remaining 80% follows 3.8.

The 20% is a reduction in the retirement claim and is sized for, not hidden.

**4.8 Project selection requires a published social cost–benefit assessment**: outcomes
expected, alternatives considered, costs, distribution of who gains, and what evidence would
show the estimate wrong. "It helps society" is not a selection criterion, because almost any
spending can be justified that way.

**4.9 Worked example.** Hospital rental receipts in Alder County accumulated in the
local-renewal account. When school repairs arose, the balance covered the cost and **no
additional AEDIS credit was issued for the work at all.** That is the intended behaviour: the
asset funded a neighbouring public need out of surplus it was already generating. A framework
that must issue fresh currency for every repair has not solved the problem; one whose assets
cover each other's renewal needs has.

## PART IV-A — MATERIALS, AND THE RESOURCE FALSE START

The original proposal began with Phase Zero: nations authorised capital directly to existing
conglomerates to "massively scale global extraction", so that materials would exist before building
began. **That sequence is withdrawn**, and this Part replaces it. The reasoning matters more than the
conclusion, because the same mistake is easy to make again.

**4.10 Why the original Phase Zero could not work.** Three facts, none of them contested:

1. **Lead times cannot be bought.** A new mine takes roughly ten to twenty years from discovery to
   first output, most of it permitting and environmental review. Credit does not compress permitting.
   Money issued for extraction capacity in year one buys metal in year twelve — and buys inflation
   immediately.
2. **Most of a project is not globally traded material.** Roads, water, sanitation, housing and school
   repair are dominated by local aggregate, cement, timber and labour. Copper, aluminium and
   specialised steel are a minority of cost, and the genuinely scarce inputs are *manufactured* —
   transformers, turbines, switchgear, cable — not mined.
3. **Paying incumbents to expand duplicates a market that already exists.** A large mining house can
   raise capital for a deposit it believes in. Public credit granted to it is a transfer to whoever
   already holds the lease, priced by the same people who would have invested anyway.

The original text also carried a political cost that was never priced: it made the framework's first
act a subsidy to the largest extractive companies in the world.

**4.11 The pipeline is published before it is funded.** Before any materials programme, the monetary
operator publishes the **aggregate requirement**: by material class, by region and by year, for the
projects already authorised and the projects forecast. **No issuance accompanies this publication.**

Aggregation is the whole mechanism. One project cannot justify a cable plant or a transformer works;
a published ten-year requirement can, and it can be contracted against. This is how national rail
programmes and offshore wind auctions already operate — the buyer publishes the volume, and industry
builds to it.

**4.12 Build first with the materials that already exist.** The programme's opening wave is chosen for
**input availability**, not for prestige:

- renewal and repair (4.5) — a school roof needs no new supply chain;
- water, sanitation and drainage — local materials dominate;
- housing and clinic construction — local materials dominate;
- labour-intensive works, which is also where 6.7's demand for workers is strongest.

**This is the inversion that answers the sequencing problem.** Build what can be built now, so that
employment and capacity arrive before any materials programme is needed. The projects that require
new mining are the ones that wait, and by waiting they stop being on the critical path.

**4.13 Contract forward for long-lead items; do not own the supply chain.** Where an input is genuinely
short — grid transformers, turbines, cable, specialised steel — the instrument is a **published
take-or-pay offtake at a stated price and volume**, not a capital grant to a supplier. The supplier
finances its own expansion against a committed order book, which is what a committed order book is
for.

Three conditions make that honest:

**An offtake is an obligation and is counted as one.** Three of four panelists objected to the
first draft, and Anthropic named the reason precisely: *an unconditional multi-year obligation is
debt in substance under 8.2.* So:

- **awarded by competitive tender**, not negotiated with one supplier;
- **signed by a named treasury**, which is the party liable if the framework does not pay;
- **counted at signing against the envelope for each delivery year**, not against the year of
  delivery — a commitment made now consumes headroom now;
- **preceded by a published 8.2 classification ruling**, because the statisticians decide what it is,
  not the drafting;
- **any commitment exceeding a future year's envelope is funded conventionally**, from the national
  budget, and the fact is published;
- the offtake is **published with its price and volume**, so nobody is negotiating quietly;
- it is **a purchase, not an equity stake** — 11.4 applies, and no revenue-contingent return is
  permitted;
- where the commitment is large relative to the supplier's capacity, the readiness gate (10.4) tests
  the price against the input price index in 4.15 before it is signed.

**4.14 Extraction is eligible only where it is local and revenue-bearing.** A mine, a smelter or a
processing plant that sells its output is **Tier 3 revenue-bearing capital** under 3.10: it earns,
and its receipts follow the 3.8 waterfall. It is therefore eligible on exactly the same terms as a
power station.

It is **never a precondition for anything else.** No project waits on a new mine. Where local material
is available and the constraint is genuinely capital, the framework can fund the extraction — and the
country keeps the asset rather than importing the finished good forever, which is the same logic as
4.6b applied one step upstream.

**4.15 Input prices govern the envelope, not just consumer prices.** The original "Material Constraint
Oracle" linked issuance to a commodity index and was withdrawn because it had no denominator. Its
concern was correct and the replacement is operational:

> **The monetary operator publishes an input price index for programme materials. Where that index
> rises faster than the basket in 3.6 for two consecutive periods, the envelope is reduced before
> further commitments are made.**

A programme large enough to matter is large enough to move its own input prices. Issuing against a
price it has just pushed up is how an infrastructure boom inflates itself, and no amount of capacity
accounting prevents it if the envelope ignores input costs.

**Gemini's objection was that the rule had no formula**, and it is right. The reduction is therefore
arithmetic: the next quarter's envelope is multiplied by **1 − (ΔI − ΔB)**, where ΔI is the trailing
twelve-month change in the input price index and ΔB is the same change in the 3.6 basket. Where the
result would fall below the protected-commitment floor in 3.6, the reduction applies to new Tier 3
approvals only and the floor is stated in the publication. The index itself, its basket and its
weights are published before activation and frozen with the 3.6 basket.

**4.16 A strategic reserve is a buffer, not a strategy.** Pre-positioning stock for the opening wave is
permitted, on three conditions: the reserve is published by class and location, it holds a stated
number of months of forecast consumption, and its acquisition goes through 4.13's offtake rules. A
reserve that grows without a release rule is a warehouse; a reserve with one is insurance against a
supply interruption the programme cannot control.

**What this Part costs, stated plainly.** The opening wave is less spectacular than the original
proposal claimed: repair, water, housing and local works rather than new mines and new grids in year
one. It is also the version that can start without waiting for a decade of permitting, and that does
not begin by paying the world's largest mining companies to do what they were going to do anyway.

**4.17 What counts as a resource.** "Resources" means three things, and the framework treats all three
the same way: **extracted material** (copper, aggregate, timber, water), **manufactured components**
(transformers, turbines, cable, switchgear, prefabricated structures, medical equipment), and **the
plant that installs them**. Manufacturing is resource production. A component that does not exist
cannot be mined, and 4.10's second fact is that the manufactured items are the real bottleneck.

**4.18 AEDIS buys the high-value material and issues it free.** For items on a **published free-issue
list** — long-lead, high-value, or safety-critical — AEDIS procures the material and owns it until it
is installed. The contractor receives it **at no charge**, and the bid covers labour, plant, overhead
and profit only. The material never enters the price.

This is standard practice in power, oil and gas, and large public works, and it exists for exactly the
reason it is needed here:

- **there is nothing to mark up**, because the contractor did not buy it;
- **there is no incentive to over-specify or over-order**, because a bigger material spend no longer
  increases the fee;
- **discounts accrue to the project**, because AEDIS is the buyer and negotiates once for the whole
  pipeline instead of once per contractor;
- **substitution is impossible**, because the specified item is the item delivered.

What the contractor carries: **custody liability** from delivery to installation, a **published waste
allowance** by material class, and an obligation to **return surplus** to the pool in 4.21.
Over-consumption beyond the allowance is at the contractor's cost, which is what keeps the material
from being treated as free in the other sense.

**What this costs AEDIS, stated plainly.** Free-issue transfers procurement risk, storage and
logistics to the framework, and it requires a competent central buyer. A jurisdiction that cannot run
a warehouse cannot run free-issue, and should begin on 4.19 instead. The clause is a capability, not a
formality.

**4.19 Bulk and local materials are reimbursed at a published reference price.** Aggregate, cement,
timber, pipe and other bulk inputs cannot sensibly be bought centrally. For those, the contractor
buys locally and is reimbursed **at a published reference price per unit, not at its own invoice.**

- the reference price is set from the ledger's own procurement record and published before bids open;
- **any discount the contractor negotiates accrues to the project**, not to the contractor;
- consumption above a published allowance is at the contractor's cost;
- the ledger holds an **open-book right**: invoices are available to the auditor on request.

**4.20 No margin on a cost AEDIS has already borne.** The rule that ties 4.18 and 4.19 together:

> **No bid may include a mark-up, handling charge, or profit on any material or component that AEDIS
> procured or whose price AEDIS has published. Bids are evaluated on labour, plant, overhead and
> profit; the material exclusion is stated in the bid documents and shown in the evaluation.**

The mark-up problem is not dishonesty, it is structure: a contractor who buys material at cost and
charges cost plus fifteen per cent earns more by spending more, and every incentive in the contract
points the wrong way. **Removing the material from the price removes the incentive**, which is cheaper
and more reliable than auditing for it afterwards.

Where a contractor does supply material — anything outside 4.18's list and 4.19's schedule — the
mark-up is **capped at a published rate**, the invoice is open-book, and the cap is disclosed in the
bid documents.

**4.21 Resources are shared between jurisdictions, on the record.** Material and plant bought by AEDIS
remain AEDIS property until consumed, and surplus does not belong to whoever happened to order it.

- **a published stock ledger**, by jurisdiction, material class and location;
- **a regional pool** for surplus and for plant — cranes, rigs, plant and vehicles move between
  projects rather than being bought twice;
- **allocation by published rule**, on need and readiness, not on who asks first;
- **the releasing jurisdiction receives a first claim on the physical pool** for material it returns,
  recorded as a stock allocation — **not a credit against its envelope.** Anthropic and Gemini both
  struck the earlier wording, correctly: a physical inventory transfer must not raise a currency
  ceiling that 3.7 reserves to the central bank alone. Ordering early is not punished because the
  claim is honoured; it is not rewarded because no headroom is created;
- **every cross-border transfer is recorded** on the ledger, with the receiving project and the
  releasing one named.

Sharing is where a multinational programme beats a national one: a transformer bought for a project
that was deferred is worth more to a neighbouring country than to a warehouse.

**4.22 Manufactured capacity is capital, and it is eligible.** A plant that makes components for the
pipeline is **Tier 3 revenue-bearing capital** under 3.10 if it sells its output, on exactly the terms
of 4.6b: it earns, and its receipts follow the 3.8 waterfall. Where a jurisdiction repeatedly imports
the same component, 4.6b's repeated-import test applies to the component as much as to the equipment.

**4.23 What this Part cannot do.** Central procurement is slower than a contractor buying locally, and
free-issue adds a failure mode — material that arrives late, or wrong, or gets lost in custody. The
ledger records **delivery dates against installation dates** for every free-issue item, and a pattern
of late delivery is a finding against the procurement operator, published like any other.


## PART V — TIER 1: CARE

**5.1 What is covered.** Necessary medical care, necessary dental care, and emergencies, for
every human, with no time limit, paid to the provider on verified delivery.

**5.2 What is not covered.** Cosmetic and elective treatment — whitening, a gold tooth, plastic
surgery performed because a patient wants it. **A cavity is covered. A gold tooth is not.
Emergency care is covered. A face lift is not.** Determination follows the classification
procedure in 7.4, not the discretion of a payer.

**5.3 Landlord and payer are separated.** An independent asset manager sets rents by published
comparators, **never by tenant turnover or retirement targets**; a separate purchaser sets
covered-care fees. Neither may set the other's terms.

**5.4 Practitioner premises are commercial tenancy.** A practitioner pays rent, pays utilities,
and buys instruments, financed through ordinary commercial credit rather than AEDIS credit.
AEDIS builds and owns the infrastructure; the practice is a commercial tenant inside it. Lease
renewal may require a minimum share of covered-care clinical hours, protecting care capacity
against an elective-only practice.

**5.5 Why this is not a patient charge.** No patient is ever charged for covered care. The
rental stream comes from the practice's own revenue, which includes the elective work a patient
pays for directly. Charging a patient for a cavity and charging a practice rent for its office
are different transactions, and the framework never conflates them.

**5.6 Provider payment discipline.** Reimbursement follows a **published fee schedule** set per
nation before activation and revised on a published cycle. **The cap is defined against the
national Tier 1 envelope, with its denominator, prospective adjustment rule and payment
forecasts published in advance.** No service is reimbursed outside the schedule. **No covered
service may be refused, and no patient charged, because of the cap.** Access outcomes — waiting
times, treatment rates and patient selection — are independently audited and published.
Removing patient bad debt is the benefit to the provider; open-ended pricing is not, and cap
pressure that produces selection or withdrawal is a failed audit. **Volume above forecast is paid at
schedule rates, on time. The purchaser recovers any excess only by prospective revision at the next
published cycle — never by withholding, late payment or retrospective reduction. Before activation
the purchaser and treasury publish and fund a contingency payment and provider-capacity schedule for
forecast envelope exhaustion.**

**5.7 Relocation support.** Where a region lacks clinicians or other essential workers, Tier 1
funds relocation, including moving costs, licensing and a published settling allowance.
Mobility is cheaper than building capacity where nobody will work.

**5.8 Disease surveillance is coupled to the ledger.** Verified outbreaks trigger accelerated
authorisation for field capacity and sanitation logistics, under the ordinary issuance rules.

## PART VI — TIER 2: WORK AND TRANSITION

**6.1 The dividend.** A monthly payment to a person in training, capped at **24 months per
verified displacement event** — resetting on a subsequent verified displacement, adjudicated
under 7.4 — indexed to a published **local cost-of-living basket** rather than a flat figure,
with published adjustments for disability and caregiving. A lifetime cap was struck because
someone displaced twice by the same technology should not be denied the transition the second
time. **Before a dividend expires, the designated national social-protection authority secures an
enforceable, funded subsistence entitlement for any recipient without adequate earnings or other
support. Payments begin without interruption and without extending Tier 2 issuance.**

The 24-month clock and the relationship between this dividend and national unemployment benefit are
governed by 6.8: training may begin on the day of displacement, benefit months do not consume
dividend months, and there is never a gap in payment between the two.

**6.2 The next tranche is contingent on progress**, verified by participation or by
deployment into work — not on attendance alone.

**6.3 What training is for.** Operating, maintaining and supervising automated systems, not only
trade skills for the assets under construction. The observed pattern is that the trained worker
becomes the person who runs the machines that replaced many: the farm labourer who manages the
picking system and carries out its repairs; the cleaner who operates cleaning robots for twelve
client companies. **Deployment measures count those roles, not construction placements alone.**

**6.4 Transition is measured honestly.** Published measures include the training-to-deployment
conversion rate, labour-force participation two years after each cohort completes, and
comparison against matched non-recipient cohorts. **No threshold in this Part is treated as evidence-based until such a comparison exists**, and the dividend is described as a time-limited transition wage, not a floor. **The comparison cohort and its matching variables are pre-registered before the first dividend payment in any cohort. A comparison constructed after the payments have been made reintroduces the survivor bias the test exists to remove, and does not count as evidence.**

**6.5 Coverage is a condition of adoption, not a cost of it.** Adoption invites backlash in
proportion to the number of people it ruins. A firm that automates inside a covered transition
keeps its licence to operate; one that automates outside it invites regulation, litigation and
sabotage.

**6.6 National unemployment protection: the state holds it, AEDIS built it.**

Where a jurisdiction has no unemployment scheme, AEDIS supplies the components and does not become
the insurer:

- **a model statute**, published and adaptable, defining contribution rates, eligibility, benefit
  formula, duration and appeals;
- **the entitlement record** on the ledger, so that contributions paid and benefits owed are
  auditable by the worker who is owed them;
- **collection rails** through payroll, with the employer remitting and the state receiving;
- **a transition supplement**, time-limited, while the national fund builds reserves. It is
  AEDIS money and it stops only on **certification by a lot-drawn auditor under 10.4 that the fund
  passes a preregistered stress test**, which must include contribution-base erosion — the case
  where automation shrinks the very payroll the scheme is funded from. "The fund can carry its own
  draw" was self-certified and therefore not a test at all;
- **audit and a published funding ratio**, so the scheme cannot be quietly drained.

**Three design constraints, each with a reason:**

1. **The pool carries a statutory non-payroll guarantee.** Payroll contributions shrink exactly
   when automation displaces the contributors, so a fund financed only by payroll is insolvent in
   the scenario it exists for. The national treasury stands behind the pool with a published
   guarantee, and where it cannot, 6.6a applies.

2. **Contribution income goes to a pooled national fund, not to individually credited accounts.**
   Individual accounts are attractive and they fail at exactly the moment insurance is needed: a
   recession draws on every account at once, so individually credited balances are empty when the
   need is greatest. Individual accounts may exist alongside a pooled floor sized to cover a
   recession-year draw, never instead of it.
2. **AEDIS does not hold the money.** Custody and collection sit with the national treasury or a
   licensed custodian. 2.5 says AEDIS takes no deposits, and an unemployment fund is a deposit
   business whatever it is called; a framework that promised not to be a bank must not become one
   by holding payroll contributions.
3. **Interest accrues to the fund and is applied to benefits**, not to individual balances. That is
   what keeps a reserve real in the years it is not being drawn.

**6.6a A backstop for everyone the scheme structurally misses.** A contribution-funded scheme
cannot pay people who never contributed: the informal sector, the long-term excluded, and anyone
whose benefits are exhausted during a displacement that outlasts the reserve. OpenAI's objection to
6.6 as drafted was that it provided no answer for them. **The treasury therefore funds a non-payroll
subsistence backstop, at the 11.1 baseline, for claimants the contribution base does not reach**,
and its cost is published annually against the funding ratio. Without this clause, 6.6 protects the
employed and abandons the rest.

**Why this belongs in the framework at all:** the standing constraint in 0.4 cannot be met by a
transfer that ends. A worker who exhausts a 24-month dividend and has no unemployment scheme behind
them is in the position 0.4 forbids. Building the scheme is cheaper than paying around its absence,
and it survives AEDIS leaving.

**6.7 AEDIS is also a labour-demand engine, and that is a claim with a falsifier.** The transition
machinery in Parts V and VI is insurance. It is written this way because insurance that turns out to
be unused is a success, not a failure of the forecast.

The forecast underneath it is a **comparison of two rates**, and it should be stated as one:

- **the rate at which AEDIS creates demand** for labour — construction, operations, maintenance,
  inspection, training, and the manufacturing that 4.6b puts where the importing is happening;
- **the rate at which automation removes it** in the sectors being automated.

**Where the first exceeds the second, unemployment pressure falls and the transition machinery goes
unused.** That is not a hope; it is the observable consequence of a project pipeline that needs human
bodies on sites that do not yet exist. A jurisdiction with a verifiable gap between funded projects
and available skilled labour experiences labour *shortage*, not surplus, and 4.6a is the clause that
says what to do about it: bring crews in, train the local workforce, and raise the local requirement
each renewal until the gap closes.

**The falsifier:** if project backlogs clear while vacancies stay unfilled, or if displacement
outpaces project creation in a jurisdiction for two consecutive years, the demand forecast is wrong
there and Parts V and VI carry the load. Both outcomes are published from the same ledger: vacancies
by trade, and displaced persons by benefit status.

**What this changes about the document.** It means the framework does not need to predict the
answer. It needs to make both outcomes survivable, and to publish which one is happening.

**6.8 Continuous income through the transition — the clock, the gap, and the vacancies.**

A displaced person should be able to walk from a lost job into training without losing a payment, and
without spending months waiting for a benefit period to expire. Four rules make that true:

**(a) Training entry is immediate and does not wait out the benefit.** A person may take up a funded
training place on the day they are displaced. Requiring someone to exhaust unemployment benefit before
training begins wastes the months in which they are most employable, and it costs more: benefit paid
while waiting is money spent on nothing, where benefit paid during training buys a qualification.

**(b) Unemployment benefit counts as satisfying the job-search condition.** In most national schemes,
entering full-time training removes you from benefit, because the scheme requires you to be actively
seeking work. **A participating jurisdiction shall amend its scheme so that an approved training place
counts as satisfying that condition.** Without this amendment the sequencing in (a) is unlawful in the
countries that need it most, and the clause becomes decoration.

**(c) The Tier 2 clock does not run while unemployment benefit is paying.** The 24 months in 6.1 are
counted from the first day the dividend is paid, not from the day of displacement. Otherwise a person
with twelve months of benefit gets twelve months of dividend, and the same displacement costs them
half their entitlement for having paid into a scheme. **Unemployment benefit months are not Tier 2
months.**

**(d) There is never a gap between the two.** Where benefit ends and a training place has not begun,
the dividend starts anyway. Where benefit and dividend overlap, one tops up the other so that total
support does not fall below the 11.1 subsistence baseline. **The payment is continuous; only the payer
changes.** A missed payment is a failed audit, and the ledger operator is the named actor.

**Training must point at a vacancy, not at a course catalogue.** No training place is funded for an
occupation with no verified demand — unless it is a capability gap under 4.6a with a named employer
commitment attached. The vacancy list is published by trade and region from the same ledger as 6.7's
falsifier, so a person choosing a course can see where the work actually is.

**6.9 Training is an asset under construction, and the subsistence duty protects it.**

Three statements, and the framework needs all three together or the accounting goes wrong:

**(a) Training is investment, not consumption.** It is classified productive under 3.1 because it
augments capacity, and it is directed at a verified vacancy (6.8) rather than at a course catalogue.
A country that trains people for work it needs is building something, and the issuance that funds it
is not the same kind of money as a war or a subsidy to an incumbent.

**(b) It produces no recoverable asset.** Human capital accrues to the worker and, over time, to the
tax base. **The framework therefore does not book it as an asset, does not retire issuance against it,
and cannot reclaim it.** No holder has a claim on a skill, and a promise to recoup training costs from
a future wage would make the worker a debtor of a programme meant to help them. This is the two-ledger
rule applied honestly: the investment is real, the return is real, and neither belongs on the balance
sheet that cancels currency.

**(c) The subsistence guarantee is therefore protection of an investment already made, not a benefit
granted.** A trainee who is abandoned halfway through a course destroys the money already spent on
them and delivers nothing: the asset is written off before it exists. That is why the continuity in
6.8(d) and the floor in 0.4 are **not discretionary**, and why they survive an envelope cut while new
Tier 3 approvals do not. Protecting an asset under construction is cheaper than scrapping it, and
every finance ministry already understands this language.

**What would show this is a costume rather than a description:** if trained cohorts show no measurable
capacity gain — no deployment into a verified vacancy, no output, no contribution to the tax base —
then the asset claim is false and Tier 2 is a transfer dressed as investment. Both series are
published from the same ledger as 6.7's falsifier, and the finding is published with the claim it
refutes.

**What this costs, and why it is cheaper.** The overlap in (d) looks like double payment and is not:
the alternative is benefit paid during a waiting period, followed by a longer benefit claim later
because the qualification arrived late. The framework's claim is narrow and testable — **the total
cost of support through to placement falls when training starts on day one**, and the ledger records
both variants where jurisdictions differ.
 The Luddites were not opposed to machinery in the abstract; they were skilled workers
whose trade was destroyed in a few years with no transition at all.

## PART VII — GOVERNANCE, TRANSPARENCY AND ADJUDICATION

**7.1 The Public Ledger.** A permissioned, federated ledger records every authorisation,
contract, payment, delivery, milestone verification, classification decision, approval, change
order and audit finding, in machine-readable form. Offline-first: transactions queue locally and synchronise, so the record cannot be silenced
by a connectivity blackout.

**7.1a Publication protects the people in the record.** The ledger publishes expenditure,
contracting and outcome data — not identities. **The public ledger carries disclosure-controlled aggregates only.** Person-level clinical and
beneficiary records are held by the care purchaser and the national social-protection authority, and
are released to an auditor only on authorisation by the 7.4 body. **Pseudonymisation was the first
draft and it is not sufficient**: Anthropic and Gemini both noted that pseudonymised person-level
records are linkable against other data, and Gemini's framing is the right one — a k-anonymity floor
on published cells, with suppression below a published minimum cell size, is a rule; a pseudonym is
a hope. **The k is published, and so is the query budget**: repeated queries against the same cells
reconstruct the individuals that a single query suppresses, so the operator publishes a budget of
overlapping releases and refuses queries that would cross it. OpenAI's point stands as a limit rather
than a solution: cell suppression alone cannot prevent linkage against external data, which is why
7.1a holds aggregates and the release route for person-level data is 7.4 authorisation, not
statistical obfuscation. Failure test: any published record permits re-identification, or a keyholder releases data
without 7.4 authorisation. OpenAI and Claude
raised this independently: a registry built to expose contractors must not expose patients, and a
health system that publishes who received care has done something worse than the corruption it was
auditing. Failure test: any public record permits re-identification of a patient or beneficiary.

**7.2 Machine-scored procurement.** Criteria and their weights are **published before bids are
opened**; every bid is scored automatically and reproducibly on the ledger. **Any award that
departs from the highest-scoring bid requires a published justification**, names its author,
and is escalated automatically to the appeal body. **A lower-scoring winner is a red flag that
raises itself** — choosing a firm plainly unsuited to the work is how these deals are done.

**7.3 AI monitoring, with human adjudication.** Monitors run continuously against published
rule sets. **The model never decides.** A published flag taxonomy states what is watched for:
bid-pattern correlation between supposedly independent suppliers; related-party links between
bidder, approver and verifier; overruns beyond a stated threshold; milestone verification
inconsistent with delivery evidence; reused or duplicated documents and photographs;
shell-company and nominee patterns; awards made outside a tender window; repeated sole-source
awards to one supplier across jurisdictions.

Every flag goes to a human adjudicator with the evidence attached. Adjudications publish
reasons and become precedents. Flag throughput is published: raised, upheld, and closed without
explanation.

Limits, published alongside the mechanism: **whoever writes the flags shapes what is visible**,
so the instruction set is public, versioned and changed only with published justification;
**false negatives are unknowable**, so the taxonomy also states what is not watched, and outside
parties may propose new patterns; **adversaries adapt**, so patterns are re-derived, rules
rotated, and an independent team is commissioned to evade the monitor and report how; and **a
flag is not a finding**, so uphold rates and closure reasons are the measure.

**7.4 Classification precedents and appeals.** Every determination — covered or elective,
productive or consumptive, renewal or operations — is published with its reasons and its
evidence. **Each new decision must follow precedent or distinguish itself from it in writing.**
Precedents bind later decisions and are appealable.

The appeal body is independent: the allocator may not appoint it, every denial carries an
attributed reason, a missed decision deadline **defaults to the applicant — except for monetary-readiness
determinations under 10.4, where an expired deadline confers no approval**, and scarce
non-clinical slots are allocated by lottery among equally eligible applicants. Randomised
access audits test for unexplained disparities.

**7.5 Transparency records. It does not prevent.** Earlier drafts claimed that backroom deals
were "rendered impossible" by the ledger. That claim is withdrawn: **a transparent record can
contain a biased decision or fabricated evidence.** What constrains capture is machine scoring
against pre-published criteria (7.2), precedent discipline (7.4), and attributable overrides —
not disclosure alone.

**7.6 Independent audit and diaspora verification.** Auditors are insulated from local pressure,
including through externally based verification nodes. Any attempted diversion creates a
permanent discrepancy on the ledger, flagged for public review.

**7.7 The Civilian Firewall.** Enforcement is economic only. During any suspension, penalties are
isolated to the governing apparatus: existing humanitarian services, dividends paid directly to
citizens, and funding for mid-construction projects continue uninterrupted. Civilian populations
are never weaponised by the corruption of their governments.

**7.8 Graduated enforcement.** Public disclosure → formal warning → partial suspension → full
suspension, with published triggers and published recovery conditions.

**7.8a The evidentiary pause, and what it may never touch.** Suspension is an instrument of
enforcement, and enforcement falls on the apparatus that failed, never on the people the framework
protects. Where a pause is triggered — two consecutive envelope breaches after the 3.6 reallocation
is exhausted, a substitution finding that is not resolved within its deadline, or an attempt to raise
a ceiling within six months of an inflation breach — the following applies:

- **the pause suspends uncommitted Tier 3 approvals only**, and **excludes renewal certified under
  4.5**, because a roof that is already leaking is not a new commitment;
- **Tier 2 intake is not paused.** It remains governed solely by 3.6's subsistence mechanics and
  6.8(d)'s continuity rule. **A reporting delay by an operator must never stop a newly displaced
  person from being paid**, and a clause that lets it do so has inverted the framework's priorities;
- **12.x-style triggers never touch 0.4b supplements, 6.6a backstops, or payments already in
  course**, per 7.7;
- **the pause names an actor, states a review deadline, and publishes the recovery condition before
  it takes effect.** A pause with no exit is a freeze, and a stalled review must lift by default
  rather than continue by default.

## PART VIII — ADOPTION

**8.1 Lawful opt-in.** Participation is by domestic constitutional and legislative process in
each nation. Earlier drafts proposed a global referendum cascade with automatic recall of
officials who blocked ratification; **that mechanism is withdrawn.** It is unenforceable across
sovereign legislatures, and presenting it to a finance ministry ends the conversation.

**8.2 The settlement and classification gate.** Before any commitment, each treasury and central bank
publishes: the payment currency; the settlement mechanism and contractor access; exchange-loss
allocation; the accounting treatment; and the lawful issuance authority. **Classification rulings are
sought and published, including adverse ones.** An Act declaring SIC "non-debt" binds no statistician,
court or rating agency, which classify by substance.

**8.2a What follows from the ruling, and what does not.** Three things the ruling settles, none of
them negotiable after publication:

- **where the ruling says the instrument is a liability, the treasury records a liability**, the
  framework's public description is corrected to match, and 8.3 applies. **The document does not get
  to keep calling it something else**;
- **the audit failure is activation without a published ruling, or a ruling suppressed because it was
  adverse.** It is not a ruling that goes against the framework. Drafting the duty the other way
  round guarantees the adverse ruling is never published, which is the outcome the rule exists to
  prevent;
- **convertibility and settlement are stated before activation**: the terms on which SIC is exchanged
  for national currency, who bears the exchange loss, and how that allocation appears under 2.6. An
  unallocated residual is a failed audit.

**8.3 Monetary financing may be prohibited, and the framework says so.** Where national or
treaty law prohibits it, AEDIS requires legal reform or conventional funding — not a
relabelling. A vehicle funded by central-bank reserves will likely consolidate on substance,
so ring-fencing is a legal argument to be tested, not assumed.

**8.4 Phased activation, not a cliff edge.** Activation proceeds with a coalition of central
banks moving together, under capital-flow monitoring, with published expiry rules for
conditional commitments. Earlier drafts required 85% of population and GDP simultaneously and
relied on "economic gravity" against holdouts; **that trigger is withdrawn** because it creates
holdout arbitrage and a pre-activation capital-flight risk. Universality is what would make the
monetary claim sound, and it is also what makes transition dangerous — that is a sequencing
problem, and coalitions are the answer.

**8.5 Participation does not require unanimity, and the framework must be honest about what
that costs.** A coalition settles into fewer currencies, against less capacity, with the
monetary claim proportionally weaker. That is a stated trade, not a hidden one.

**8.5a A member may leave, and leaving does not cut anyone off.** Anthropic's objection to the
first draft was decisive: *"collateral demanded before exit is demanded of a state already
leaving."* A schedule promised by a departing government is not security.

**The reserve is therefore escrowed from accession, not at exit**, built on a published schedule,
held by a custodian outside the withdrawing member's jurisdiction, and sized to the **net present
value of the trailing ten years of Tier 1 commitments at that jurisdiction** — because Gemini is
right that the net present value of a *permanent* commitment is infinite, and a horizon has to be
named for the number to exist. The discount rate is the published sovereign curve of the member at
accession, fixed for the life of the schedule so the reserve cannot be resized by moving the rate.
**A shortfall on exit is a liability of the withdrawing member, enforceable by the custodian**; if it
does not pay, the shortfall is published and 7.8's graduated enforcement applies, with 7.7
guaranteeing that care in payment continues throughout. On exit the run-off is paid from the escrow by the
custodian, so no salary depends on the departing government's goodwill on the day it leaves. Gemini
and DeepSeek reached the same conclusion independently. The coalition publishes an annual universality
review with an expansion trigger, so that the cost of partial membership is visible rather than
assumed. OpenAI, Claude and DeepSeek each raised the failure mode: a state that exits on a Tuesday
must not be able to stop a clinician's salary on the Wednesday.

## PART IX — INDUSTRY TRANSITION AND LEGACY INTERESTS

**9.1 Conversion, not expropriation.** Where private capacity exists — hospitals, clinics,
utilities — AEDIS **contracts it at a published tariff before building anything.** A facility
with spare capacity receives a volume guarantee. No asset is seized; it is employed.

**9.2 Buy rather than compete.** Where capacity must expand, existing facilities may be
purchased at independently verified value: a fair exit instead of a slow decline.

**9.3 The offer is explicit.** Compete with capacity that does not need a margin and the asset
decays; contract with the framework and it is protected. That is not expropriation — it is a
market with a new entrant that does not need to profit from scarcity.

**9.4 Legacy industries may bid.** Defence and extractive firms may convert to productive work
under the productive-issuance standard: deep-sea and civilian space research, geothermal
drilling, materials and manufacturing.

**9.5 Land value capture is a release condition.** Before any tranche is released for a
land-sensitive asset — transit, ports, grids, schools, hospitals — the jurisdiction must enact
and record on the ledger a binding uplift-capture levy. **Receipts enter the 3.8 waterfall at step 4
for revenue-bearing assets, and at step 3 for non-revenue assets — which have no waterfall of their
own under 3.10 and would otherwise leave the levy with no lawful destination.**
Public money may not become a private windfall by default, and this is not a recommendation to
be considered later.

**9.6 No civil immunity, struck by unanimous sign-off.** All three panelists who reached
this clause struck it, for the same reason: an agreement between a government and a firm cannot
lawfully extinguish the claims of victims who are not party to it. The clause is therefore
**withdrawn**. Past conduct remains with the ordinary courts of the jurisdiction concerned.

**9.6a Cooperation remains available and carries no immunity.** A transitional cooperation
agreement may still be offered. It requires full public disclosure of the conduct concerned, is
time-limited, is recorded on the ledger with clawback for violation, and **expressly preserves
every third party's right to restitution and civil recovery**. Negotiating authorities may not
waive those rights, and any agreement purporting to do so is void to that extent.

DeepSeek would go further and prohibit civil immunity outright while requiring a legacy firm to
escrow 100% of verified disputed liabilities before it may bid. That is recorded as a dissent, not
adopted: escrow of that kind may be unworkable for firms whose liabilities are genuinely unknown,
and the preservation of third-party rights already denies the benefit the clause was written to
grant.

## PART X — RESEARCH AND EVIDENCE

**10.1 No capital on theory alone.** Researchers submit testing blueprints in advance. In R&D the
completed infrastructure is the resulting data: **the final tranche is released** when results — a
pass, or a preregistered negative result that meets the methods agreed in advance — are published
and verified. **"Capital clears" was the wrong verb**, and OpenAI and Gemini both caught what it
implied: 3.5 says completion clears nothing, so a clause saying publication clears capital suggests
publishing research extinguishes currency. It does not. A milestone is paid; the money is not
cancelled. **"Mathematically demonstrated failure" was too narrow**: most empirical research returns
a valid null result, not a proof of impossibility, and the earlier wording would have left sound
negative findings unpaid. That is how a funding body teaches its researchers to report only
successes.

**10.2 Gatekeeping by published criteria and track record.** Approvers are selected by published
criteria, assessed on applied problem-solving, and rotated. Their decisions are published with
rationale, and sustained approval of failed testing parameters suspends their authority. **All
gatekeeper assessments are published**; no single test score is treated as qualification.

**10.3 Live telemetry.** Start times, end times and raw data are published as testing proceeds.
Every board vote and rationale is signed and auditable.

**10.4 Pre-activation readiness gate.** Ratification may proceed, but **monetary activation
requires independently audited testing of the executable accounting, advance-payment,
issuance-control and stabilisation rules.** Acceptance thresholds and the determination deadline
are published **before** testing; assessors are drawn by lot from a pre-qualified public pool and
named on the ledger; each failure must cite the published threshold breached; **missing the
deadline confers no approval.** Simulation is not proof of safety — it is a prerequisite for
rejecting demonstrably unsafe designs.

**10.5 Resource survey is a regional facility, not a national one.** Geology does not follow
borders, and a survey institute in every country would be 190 duplications of the same work. **One
facility per geological region**, shared by the countries in it, is the unit — with data sovereignty
retained by each state and a published agreement on who may use the facility and on what terms. A
country with no facility uses its region's; a region with none is the funding gap.

**The work is methods, not a repeat survey.** National geological surveys and international
programmes already hold most of the data. The programme funds what does not exist: new sensing,
better inversion and modelling, calibration against known deposits, and coverage where none has been
collected. Re-surveying what is already mapped is the most expensive way to learn nothing.

**10.6 Pilots gate scaling, not starting.** An external reviewer proposed making three pilot
jurisdictions, each running twenty-four months of active issuance, a precondition for any Tier 3 wave.
Four reviewers examined it and none accepted it as drafted, for one reason stated three ways: it makes
evaluation a precondition for beginning rather than for growing, and a framework that cannot begin
cannot be evaluated.

What survives is the principle, with the sequencing corrected:

- **pilots begin after 10.4's readiness gate, not instead of it.** The first authorised jurisdiction
  is a pilot by definition, and its evaluation is what licenses the second;
- **selection is by lot from the 10.4 assessor pool, within a capacity class**, never by a treasury
  nominating its own showcase. One high-capacity and one low-capacity jurisdiction must be evaluated
  before scaling beyond the published exposure threshold in Appendix D;
- **a no-capital pilot is required alongside the capital ones**, covering Tier 1 and Tier 2 only,
  because the transition machinery is the part that must work when the building does not;
- **Tier 3 pilot sign-off comes only after twelve months of post-handover operation**, when receipts
  exist. Sign-off before receipts is a forecast wearing a certificate;
- **evaluation thresholds are pre-registered and the evaluator is lot-drawn**, per 6.4's comparison
  rule extended from cohorts to jurisdictions. Post-hoc construction of a comparison, or alteration
  of a primary metric after the first payment, is a failed audit;
- **the evaluator publishes within ninety days of the pilot's end date**, with replication code and
  disclosure-controlled aggregates under 7.1a. **Person-level replication requires 7.4 authorisation.**
  A late report confers no approval and no pause: it is a failure of the evaluator, recorded as such;
- **the measured basket is the full frozen 3.6 basket, by income decile, under 11.1** — not only the
  items the programme claims to affect. A programme that selects its own scorecard has not been
  measured, and the items it excludes are exactly where a substitution hides.

**10.6 The methods are public; the deposit data belongs to the state.** Everything the programme
learns about *how* to find resources — instruments, processing, models, calibration, and the failures
— is published in full. **Deposit-level findings are held by the state and released on published
terms**, recorded on the ledger, because an uncontested public map of an unclaimed deposit is an
invitation to a rush that benefits whoever moves fastest. The state may publish; nobody else may
pre-empt it.

**10.7 The least-damage standard, with a number attached.** "Least damage" is not a sentiment, and
without a measurement it is unenforceable:

- **baseline environmental measurement before authorisation**, taken by an assessor the applicant
  does not pay;
- a **restoration bond sized to audited restoration cost**, released only on certified completion;
- a **published comparison of extraction methods per tonne of output**, by land disturbance, water
  use and contamination risk;
- **Gemini's objection was decisive**: minimising three independent variables with no exchange rate
  between them has no unique solution, and wherever one method trades lower water use against higher
  land disturbance the rule cannot answer. So the three are combined:
  **before bids open the independent environmental assessor publishes fixed scarcity-and-toxicity
  weights that normalise land disturbance, water use and contamination risk into a single composite
  impact score per tonne**, and the authorisation selects the specification-compliant method with the
  lowest composite score;
- **no method may win on composite score while falling below the regulatory minimum on any single
  measure** — a weighted average must not be able to launder a fatal defect in one dimension;
- the weights, the score and the rejected alternatives are published with the authorisation.

**Falsifier:** measured disturbance per tonne rises after the programme begins, or restoration bonds
are released without certification. Either is a failed audit, published like any other.

**10.8 Machinery is research, and it is a priority.** More efficient and safer extraction is a
capital-goods problem: better machines disturb less land for the same output, and the improvement
compounds across every project that uses them. Funding runs under 10.1 — **the deliverable is data,
and the data includes designs that failed**, which is most of them.

Three conditions:

- **publication is required**, including negative results, so that a machine that works becomes
  manufacturable by anyone under 4.6b and 4.22 rather than proprietary to one supplier;
- **a machine that works is not a monopoly**: the specification is published and open to any
  qualified manufacturer;
- **the test is per tonne, not per unit**: cost and disturbance measured against the machinery it
  replaces, on the same site class. A machine that is cheaper to buy and worse per tonne has not
  improved anything.

**Falsifier:** no measurable improvement in cost or disturbance per tonne across the programme's
first five machinery projects.

**Publication covers the design record as well as the artefact** — the problem noticed, the
constraint that forced the change, what was changed and its measured effect, not only the finished
machine. An artefact released without its rationale teaches a competitor what to copy and nobody why
it works.

**10.9 What artificial intelligence cannot do here yet, and the test that would change the answer.**
Machine systems are not currently reliable at CAD-grade engineering design: they produce plausible
geometry, not certified assemblies, and the gap between a plausible design and a manufacturable one is
where most of the cost lives. **The programme must not assume that gap closes on any particular
schedule.**

So the clause funds the workflow rather than the hope, and states the test:

> **Designs are human-led with machine assistance until a design produced with AI assistance beats
> the human-led baseline on cost, safety and life-cycle performance in blind assessment by engineers
> who did not produce either.** The comparison is preregistered and published, pass or fail.

If that test is passed, the finding is published and the workflow changes. If it is not, the
programme has still built the pipeline that would use it. **What would show this whole Section is waste:** if, across the programme's first five years, designs
produced with the corpus and the loop do not beat the baseline on cost, safety or life-cycle
performance, then the corpus is a library nobody uses and the loop is a toy. Published. The same
falsifier as 10.8 and 10.9, applied to the same claim: that capturing *how* people improve things is
worth more than buying the improvements.

## PART XI — WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS FAIL

**11.1 The test of the whole programme.** **Does the real cost of essentials fall faster than the
currency dilutes?** If not, AEDIS has failed and 0.6 requires saying so. It is measured rather
than asserted: an auditor drawn by lot under 10.4 publishes annually, using the frozen basket in
3.6 and published methods applied equally to existing programmes, **affordability, deprivation,
service access and inflation incidence by income decile, region and displacement status.**
Aggregate improvement that conceals persistent deprivation in any decile is a failed audit, not
a partial success.

**11.2 The operating cliff.** An asset whose budget cannot staff it becomes a ruin. Every rule in
this document assumes assets remain staffed; the honest consequence is **fewer hospitals where a
treasury cannot staff them**, and that consequence is stated here rather than discovered later.

**11.3 Capitalisation is partly a shadow of what it claims.** Seigniorage is not free, and the
incidence falls on cash holders, fixed-income recipients, and the poor through essentials prices.

**11.4 Private capital may build, and may not own.** Earlier versions recorded this as an
unresolvable tension: investors funding AEDIS assets would lobby for project selection and bend
the pipeline toward what pays. It is resolved by separating doing the work from owning the thing.
**Private participation is limited to competitively procured, fixed-price contracts** for
construction (3.4), renewal (4.5), research data (10.1) and operations (3.8). **Private equity,
debt financing, selection rights, and any return contingent on an asset's tariff, revenue or
value are prohibited**, and no such contractor may propose or score selection.

Falsifier: approved grids and desalination plants track contractor order books more closely than
they track need registers.

**11.5 There is no world sovereign.** Permanent global care requires a multinational issuer with
real authority, and no such institution exists. Part VIII's coalition approach is a workaround,
not a solution.

**11.6 No calibrated threshold exists.** The ratios in circulation are placeholders. Any statement
that a particular ratio is evidence-based would be false.

**11.7 The premise may be wrong, and it is judged on the displaced, not the survivors.** If real
wages in exposed occupations rise for a decade while automation spreads, the case in 0.3
collapses; that would be the most valuable finding of all, and it would require ending the framework's displacement provisions rather than defending
them. **Ending them is a wind-down, not a stop**: care already in payment continues under the 8.5a
run-off, and 7.7 applies throughout. Anthropic's point — *"'abandoning the framework' would stop care
already being paid for"* — is the difference between admitting a premise was wrong and punishing
people for it. **Occupational averages cannot establish it, because they
include the people who left.** The test runs on matched displaced cohorts under 6.4 against
preregistered comparators. A framework judged on the earnings of those still employed is
measuring the wrong population.

**11.8 The veto must be testable.** Judge AEDIS by whether an envelope has ever actually been
refused and whether approvals have ever been frozen under 4.4; and, where no breach has occurred, by
**independently witnessed exercises that operate the controls under stress and publish the result**.
**A framework with an untested veto is not a constrained framework.** A breach-free decade is only
evidence if the controls were exercised and passed — a compliant decade with untested controls is the
old institution with a ledger.

**11.9 The framework will be opposed, and the arguments are known.** A proposal that creates money and
changes who allocates it will be resisted by the institutions whose function it changes. Pretending
otherwise is the least credible thing this document could do. The strongest arguments against it, and
what answers each:

| The objection | Its strongest form | What answers it |
|---|---|---|
| **Inflation** | Issuance against capacity assumes capacity arrives; the interim is unbacked | 3.4's staged release, 3.6's withdrawal instruments, 3.7's envelope set by the central bank alone. Not answered by argument — answered by 10.4's gate |
| **Fiscal dominance** | A central bank that finances public works has lost its independence | 3.7 gives the envelope to the central bank alone; 2.6 allocates every settlement cost; 8.2 requires published classification rulings, including adverse ones |
| **Moral hazard** | Cheap money for public works funds whatever is politically convenient | 4.8's cost-benefit test, 4.12's input-availability rule, 7.2's machine scoring, 7.4's precedents |
| **Picking winners** | A committee cannot allocate capital better than a market | 4.13 buys forward rather than investing; 11.4 forbids ownership and revenue-contingent returns; 4.6b responds to an observed import record rather than a forecast |
| **Exit and contagion** | A member leaving takes its commitments with it | 8.5a escrows from accession with a custodian outside the member's jurisdiction |
| **Political capture** | The ledger becomes a tool of whoever controls it | 0.6's publication duty, 7.1a's aggregates, 7.4's appeal body that the allocator cannot appoint |

**Every one of those answers is a clause, not a reassurance.** Where an objection is not answered by a
clause, this document says so rather than implying otherwise: 11.4's capture question was resolved by
prohibition, 6.6's funding was not, and the placeholder register in Appendix D admits that the
calibration does not exist.

**On the comparative claim, stated carefully.** The document does not claim AEDIS is riskless. It
claims the risk of not acting is larger, and that claim has to be measurable or it is rhetoric.

**The first draft of this paragraph was circular and OpenAI and Gemini both caught it.** Pricing the
avoided deficit at replacement cost inflates the benefit by the very input demand the programme
creates: the more AEDIS builds, the more expensive replacement becomes, and the more valuable its own
absence looks.

The comparison is therefore stated so that it cannot flatter the framework:

> **Incremental service benefits against real resource costs, distributional harms and financing
> effects** — assessed by an independent auditor, under preregistered feasible alternatives, with
> **replacement expenditure not counted as benefit** and transfers not counted twice.

**Falsifier:** if credible out-of-sample estimates show AEDIS is persistently dominated by a feasible
alternative, the framework is a worse trade and is **wound down under 11.7** — not stopped, because
11.7 already says that care in payment continues. That is a reasonable thing for its opponents to
demand, and it is the measurement to ask for instead of a forecast.

**And one honest note on the politics.** An institution that will lose authority under this framework
is not being unreasonable to oppose it, and describing that opposition as corruption is both wrong and
counterproductive. The argument to make is the one in the table: which clause answers which objection,
and what would have to be measured to settle it. A proposal that can be argued with is harder to dismiss than one that can only be believed.

**11.10 The falsification list, with windows and margins fixed in advance.** The pause triggers in
7.8a are immediate. The following are grounds for a formal redesign recommendation, assessed on
pre-registered adjustment windows and effect margins, and every one of them must distinguish
**insufficient evidence from demonstrated failure** — an underpowered null result establishes nothing
and may never be cited as one:

- the real cost of the covered essential basket fails to fall at least as fast as the measured
  dilution of the currency, after controls that are **quantified and pre-registered**, not asserted
  after the fact;
- training-to-deployment conversion remains statistically indistinguishable from matched non-recipient
  cohorts across two successive cohorts;
- substitution findings recur and are repeatedly cured only by redefining the metric or its
  denominator;
- capture of the ledger operator, the assessor pool or the monetary-operator interface by a single
  treasury or contractor, measured by appointment concentration or by suppression of adverse findings.
  **Capture suspends that operator's new discretionary approvals. It is not a hard stop**, because
  stopping payments is the capture's victory, not its remedy.

A redesign recommendation proceeds as a wind-down under 11.7, with care in payment continuing
throughout.

## APPENDIX A — DEFINITIONS

- **SIC (Sovereign Infrastructure Credit):** an authorisation to issue currency for verified
  productive capacity or permanent care. **Not a debt instrument.** No maturity, no interest, no
  redemption right.
- **Public Ledger:** the permissioned federated record described in 7.1.
- **Envelope:** the ceiling on issuance for a currency area, sized to measured absorption.
- **Monetary operator:** the central bank of the currency area, or where several share one, the joint
  body they have designated in writing and published. The term carries the 3.7 powers — setting and
  enforcing the envelope, and publishing the input price index — and no other body may exercise them.
  Anthropic's reservation was that the document used the term beside "the central bank alone" without
  saying they were the same thing.
- **Construction exposure:** peak unbacked circulating stock during build (3.5).
- **Terminal clearing horizon:** outstanding stock at handover ÷ annual cancellation (3.5).
- **Revenue-bearing / non-revenue asset:** classification defined in 3.10.
- **Productive / consumptive issuance:** classification defined in 3.1.
- **Covered / elective care:** classification defined in 5.1–5.2, adjudicated under 7.4.

## APPENDIX F — ON REPRODUCIBILITY

The verification round was run twice against the same document, with the same questions and the
same models. Clause citations overlapped by roughly 60% across the two runs, and one panelist
reversed its verdict on clause 3.7 between them — satisfied at 13:48, broken at 16:05 — without any
change to the text.

Three consequences, and they should be read as a property of the method rather than a defect:

1. **A single review is one sample, not a finding.** What survives a repeat is signal; what appears
   once is a hypothesis.
2. **Self-reported authorship is unreliable.** Asked which clauses it had authored, one model named
   3.7, 3.8 and 11.4 in the first run and 3.6, 3.7 and 11.7 in the second. Panelists do not hold a
   record of who proposed what.
3. **The repeat was worth running.** It found three refinements the first pass missed — the baseline
   schedule in 1.4, the third breach step in 3.6, and pre-registration in 6.4 — each closing a
   specific evasion rather than restating a complaint.

A framework that publishes its own variance is more credible than one that presents a single
verification as settled. This appendix exists so that no reader mistakes one round for a verdict.

**12.7 was named as the most damaging clause by all four reviewers, unanimously.** It suspended new
Tier 2 intakes on triggers including a late publication — an administrative failure by an operator,
punished by withholding transition income from a newly displaced person. 0.4 forbids that outcome, 7.7
confines penalties to the apparatus, and 3.6 gives the central bank alone the authority a pause was
bypassing. **It was rejected, and the surviving version pauses only uncommitted Tier 3 approvals.**

**The most valuable item was 12.6, and it was amended rather than accepted.** The draft froze one
real-intensity metric per function. Three reviewers found the same hole: the definition is frozen but
the *choice* is not, and a treasury that picks its own metric has not been measured. It now requires a
lot-drawn auditor to hold **two** frozen metrics — one volume, one access — with need adjustment, a
sixty-day substitution finding rather than a suspension, and a historical-bridge rule for statistical
revisions. The draft's other half, freezing the 3.7 denominator, was adopted as 3.7a.

**12.8 was sent into 8.2 by three of four, and the reason is the sharpest thing in the review.** The
draft made an adverse classification ruling a failed audit. Anthropic named the trap: *a rule under
which an adverse ruling fails the audit is a rule that guarantees the adverse ruling is never
published.* The duty was reversed — the failure is **activation without a published ruling**, or a
ruling suppressed for being adverse. The draft's convertibility requirement was kept as 8.2a.

**Two reviewers added a pilot the draft had missed.** Anthropic, Gemini and OpenAI all required a
**no-capital Tier 1 and Tier 2 pilot**, because the transition machinery is the part that has to work
when the building does not.

**DeepSeek accepted eight of ten and rejected the two that matter**, on the ground that *"pilots begin
with first authorisation; evaluation gates scaling, not start."* That sentence is the clearest
statement in the round of why 12.2 as drafted would have stalled the framework.

**What this round shows about the document.** An outside reviewer produced eleven hundred lines of
careful procedure, and the panel's productive contribution was not to add to it but to ask who the
procedure punishes. Every clause that survived was rewritten to point at the apparatus rather than the
beneficiary — which is the same correction the panel made to 0.4a, to 6.6, and to 9.6 before it.
