# AEDIS — Advanced Economic Development and Infrastructure System

**A proposal for building productive capacity without borrowing, and for carrying the people a
transition displaces. Published with its objections attached.**

This repository contains one document: [`AEDIS-PROPOSAL.md`](AEDIS-PROPOSAL.md). It is long because
the objections are inside it rather than missing from it.

---

## What AEDIS proposes

Most infrastructure is paid for with debt, and debt is a claim on future revenue. AEDIS proposes the
alternative: **issuance against capacity, not borrowing against the future.**

Four commitments hold it together:

1. **Seigniorage, not debt.** Issuance has no creditor, no interest and no maturity — so it never
   creates a claim on a future budget. It creates currency against capacity instead.
2. **Productive issuance only.** Every issuance is classified at approval as productive
   (asset-forming, capacity-augmenting, income-producing now or later) or consumptive. Only the first
   is eligible. War, patronage and legacy debt service are ineligible by name.
3. **A hard ceiling with cancellation capacity.** A central bank sets an envelope sized to measured
   absorption, and must hold executable instruments to withdraw liquidity if activation does not
   arrive. **An envelope is not a promise to spend; it is a ceiling that can be enforced.**
4. **A standing constraint that outranks the program.** No person displaced by automation may be
   left with nothing left to lose. National unemployment protection is the first layer, the AEDIS
   transition dividend is the second, and where a country has no scheme, AEDIS helps build one (6.6)
   rather than paying around its absence.

The delivery structure is three tiers: **permanent care** (Tier 1, publicly funded), a
**24-month-per-displacement transition dividend** (Tier 2), and **asset-linked capital** (Tier 3),
where revenue-bearing assets retire their issuance from receipts and non-revenue assets do not.

## How this document was produced

This is the part worth scrutinising, because it is the part that is unusual.

The document was reviewed by four frontier models — OpenAI GPT-6 Astra Pro, Anthropic Claude Fable
5.2, Google Gemini 4 Argon and DeepSeek V4 Pro — across fifteen sessions and **257 aired turns**.

**The referee is not a language model.** Turn order, word limits, time limits, the round structure and
the transcript are all enforced by deterministic code. No model decides who speaks, when a round ends,
or whether a claim is admissible. That matters: a model acting as judge can be talked into agreement,
and a debate where the referee is also a participant produces the answer the referee prefers.

Each model was given the same question and the same word cap, and told the speech is cut at the limit.

**The result is not consensus, and it was never meant to be.** Four models asked whether they agree
will agree. So each sign-off asked a different question: state a position per Part, name the clause
you would strike, name the clause you would defend, and name the one clause you still cannot accept.

Three sign-offs are recorded verbatim in the document — **Appendix G, H and I** — and each one names
its dissent rather than averaging it away. The final sign-off cost $2.35 and returned eight of eight
turns.

### What that process actually caught

The reviews found defects the author had not seen, including several in clauses the author had written
himself. A sample, from Appendix H and I:

- A gate requiring a funded unemployment scheme to exist before transition support opened — **struck by
  all three models that reached it**, because it left people unsupported in exactly the countries least
  able to build a scheme. Compliance with the framework would have been the reason someone had nothing.
- An unemployment fund that could **certify its own readiness**, and that was financed only by payroll —
  insolvent precisely when automation shrinks the payroll.
- "Capital clears" when research is published, which contradicted the rule that completion clears
  nothing, and implied publishing a paper cancels currency.
- A multi-year supply offtake counted as a purchase rather than an obligation. *"An unconditional
  multi-year obligation is debt in substance."*
- Minimizing land disturbance, water use and contamination with no exchange rate between them — a
  rule with no unique solution. Wherever one method traded water for land, it could not answer.
- A comparative metric that priced the avoided deficit at replacement cost, which is circular: the
  program's own input demand inflates the replacement cost, and therefore inflates the value of its
  own absence.

Each is recorded in the document with the fix, and with the model that raised it.

## What is not finished, and where this is weak

A proposal that hides its limits is not a proposal, it is a sales document. The document states these
itself, and they are repeated here because they are the honest answer to "why should I trust this":

- **The calibration does not exist.** Retirement ratios, provider caps, tax rates, the dividend amount
  and the subsistence baseline are all **placeholders**, listed in **Appendix D** with a producer, a
  source and a deadline for each. No figure may be used operationally while it remains undefined.
- **Nothing here has been piloted.** There is no jurisdiction test, no operating history, no measured
  outturn. What exists is a design that has been attacked hard and survived with its reservations
  recorded.
- **Four reservations remain unresolved** by design rather than by oversight: provider cap calibration
  (5.6), the AI-design baseline (10.9), the regional survey body (10.5), and transition thresholds
  (6.4). They need calibration, not drafting.
- **One dissent was never adopted.** DeepSeek would strike the 80% local-labor preference (4.6) as a
  subsidy to incumbents. The author kept it, with an auditor's value-added ruling that voids it where
  it fails its own test, and the dissent stands on the record.

**The document also names the arguments against it (11.9)** — inflation, fiscal dominance, moral
hazard, picking winners, exit and contagion, political capture — and answers each with a clause rather
than a reassurance. Where an objection is not answered by a clause, it says so.

And it carries a falsifier that cuts against itself: **if credible out-of-sample estimates show AEDIS
is persistently dominated by a feasible alternative, it is wound down under 11.7** — not stopped,
because care already in payment continues.

## What came from the author rather than the panel

Worth stating plainly, because "generated by AI" is not an accurate description of this document:

- **The revenue-bearing asset rule** — issuance retires from receipts on assets that sell output —
  which broke a deadlock the panel could not get past.
- **The unemployment protection layer** (0.4a, 0.4b, 6.6): existing national schemes come first, AEDIS
  funds the gap, and a scheme is built where none exists.
- **Continuous income through transition** (6.8, 6.9): training begins on the day of displacement,
  benefit months do not consume dividend months, and the guarantee is protection of an asset under
  construction rather than a benefit.
- **The labor-demand argument** (4.6a, 4.6b, 6.7): cross-border crews on a capability gap with a
  transfer obligation that abolishes itself, manufacturing built where the importing happens, and the
  claim that AEDIS expands work faster than automation removes it — stated as a rate comparison with a
  falsifier, not as a promise.

Everything else is the panel's corrections applied to the author's design. The document records which
is which.

## Status and how to respond

**Status: a design under review. Not a pilot, not a policy, not a claim that it will work.**

The most useful responses, in order:

1. **A clause that cannot be implemented as written.** Cite the number. That is the single most useful
   thing anyone can send.
2. **A placeholder you can fill.** Appendix D lists every undefined figure with the body that should
   produce it. A defensible number with a source is worth more than an opinion.
3. **A feasible alternative that dominates this one** — the 11.9 falsifier exists for exactly that
   argument, and it is the argument most likely to be right.

Disagreement is more useful than agreement here, and the document was built to survive it.
